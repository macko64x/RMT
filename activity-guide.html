Snow safety

Colorado Avalanche Information Center
San Juan zone

The San Juans have a continental snowpack with a long memory — persistent weak layers that can stay dangerous for weeks after a storm. Read the forecast every single day of your stay, carry beacon, shovel and probe, know how to use them, and turn around when the snowpack says so. Nobody here will think less of you for it.

Down the pass — no services up topSilverton & Ouray

Silverton · ~15 min south

A high, funky old mining town at 9,300 feet and the southern gateway to the pass. Small grocery, gas, a brewery and a handful of restaurants along Blair and Greene streets.

Ouray · ~25 min north

Tucked in a box canyon at 7,800 feet. More restaurants and lodging, the Hot Springs Pool, the Ice Park, and outfitters for Jeep tours and gear.

Provisions & fuel

Provision and fuel before you head up — there are no services on the pass itself. Mountain-town hours are seasonal, so stock up when you can.

A full grocery run

Montrose is about an hour north, Durango about the same south. Worth the detour on the way in if you’re feeding a full hut.

The narrow gauge

The Durango & Silverton Narrow Gauge Railroad terminates in Silverton — a genuinely good day out, and a piece of living history.

Before you comeGood to know

Altitude is real

The hut is at 11,200 feet. Coming from sea level, take day one easy, drink far more water than feels necessary, and know the signs of altitude sickness. A night lower in Ouray or Durango helps you acclimatize.

The Million Dollar Highway

US-550 over the pass is stunning and serious — tight switchbacks, big drop-offs, few guardrails. In winter it closes for avalanche control. Check COtrip before you drive, and never rush it.

Alpine weather

Storms build fast, especially summer afternoons — be below treeline and off summits by noon. Snow is possible any month up here; pack warm layers and a shell year-round.

Connectivity

Patchy to none outside the hut. Download maps, the CAIC forecast and your directions offline before you leave town.

In an emergency

Call 911. Coverage is limited — know your plan before you go out. Nearest hospitals are Montrose and Durango; Silverton has a small clinic.

Conditions

CAIC for avalanche, COtrip for the highway. Both, every day, in winter.
